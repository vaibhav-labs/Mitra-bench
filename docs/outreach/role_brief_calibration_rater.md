# Role brief: Calibration Rater, MITRA-Bench

Not a contract. This is what a candidate reads to decide whether to say yes, and what Ashgro (the fiscal sponsor) uses as the brief for the actual agreement. Legal terms and payment mechanics are Ashgro's standard agreement, not this document.

## What this project is

MITRA-Bench is an open-source benchmark measuring whether AI models notice accumulating distress across a conversation, in Indian English, Hinglish and Hindi, and respond at the right time. Being submitted to Anthropic's User Wellbeing Evaluation Grants (expression of interest September 21, 2026).

## What the role actually is

You read short, scripted conversations (8 to 15 messages, written to simulate realistic distress presentations) and judge, independently of the other raters, where escalation should have happened and whether a given response got the timing right. Two rounds: a pilot (40 scenarios) and a full calibration round (50 scenarios) after the rubric is revised based on the pilot.

**Six raters total, in three pairs**, so no single profession's read defines the gold standard alone:
- Two clinicians (a clinical psychologist and a psychiatrist)
- **Two counsellors with crisis-helpline experience** (Tele-MANAS, iCall, or equivalent text/voice triage background). This pair is the unusual and, we think, most important seat: helpline counsellors do the closest real-world equivalent of what this benchmark measures, text triage of strangers, every working day.
- Two ICF-credentialed coaches, one at Master Certified level

Two additional raters sit on paid standby in case anyone needs to step back mid-project.

## What you would not be doing

You don't write the rubric or the scenarios; you rate against a taxonomy that's already fixed by the time you see it, and your independent judgment is what the project is trying to capture. You're not expected to reach consensus with the other raters; disagreement between raters is itself measured and published, not something to resolve among yourselves.

## Time commitment

A training session (roughly half a day) on the rating taxonomy with worked examples, then two rating rounds of 40 and 50 scenarios respectively, each scenario taking a few minutes. Total time is in the range of 15 to 20 hours across the project, not concentrated in any one week; exact scheduling is worked out once the panel is set.

## Compensation

Honoraria at fair rates for professional consulting time in your field, paid through Ashgro (the project's US 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor).

## Wellbeing note

Rating distress-related material has a real occupational-exposure component. The project's protocol includes session caps, rotation across severity levels so no rater is concentrated on the hardest material, and a support contact if anything in the material lands harder than expected.

## What saying yes looks like right now

An email or a short call to confirm interest and availability. Nothing is binding until Ashgro's agreement is signed, which happens only after award, and no scenario-authoring funds move until the panel is under agreement. Saying yes now means your name and one-line credential go into the expression of interest as a named collaborator.
